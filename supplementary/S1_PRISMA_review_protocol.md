# S1. Detailed PRISMA-informed review protocol

## 2.1 Methodology

Establishing the research gap addressed by this study requires more than a narrative account of prior work. It requires a structured assessment of whether studies situated at the terminological intersection of AR/MR, AI/DL-based inspection, and HITL document these components as a functionally integrated and persistently learning architecture. To this end, this section adopts a lightweight, PRISMA 2020-informed review protocol (Page et al., 2021). The protocol is proportionate to its supporting role in this paper and does not constitute an exhaustive standalone systematic review. The complete screening pipeline, including the retrieved records, phase-by-phase decisions, and article-level classifications, is available in a public repository ([anonymized repository link] to support reproducibility.

Four databases were used to identify and cross-verify the literature. Scopus and IEEE Xplore were selected for their broad and domain-specific peer-reviewed engineering/computer-science coverage, respectively. ScienceDirect served as the primary venue for maintenance-engineering and applied-Artificial Intelligence (AI) journals relevant to this study. Google Scholar was used as a grey-literature and recall backstop, to capture preprints, theses, and non-Elsevier/IEEE venues (arXiv, MDPI, technical reports) not otherwise indexed. Grey literature was deliberately included to capture contributions still in the publication pipeline. Because Google Scholar's coverage is unfiltered by editorial or peer-review status, grey literature identified through it was retained only if it had a recognized issuing body (industry, government, or professional association), a described methodology with a minimum standard of rigor, and a stable, permanently accessible identifier (DOI or archival record). The bibliographic search was conducted on 02 July 2026, covering the period 2017–2026.

The review is structured around six research questions, formulated using a PICOC framework (Population: industrial/infrastructure maintenance and visual inspection systems; Intervention: integration of AR/Mixed Reality (MR), deep-learning (DL)-based defect detection/segmentation, and HITL feedback; Comparison: terminological co-occurrence or partial functional coupling of AR/MR, AI/DL, and human involvement versus their documented functional integration within a single inspection workflow; Outcomes: architectural maturity, pose/metadata traceability, feedback persistence, hierarchical multi-model design, reported return on investment (ROI); Context: industrial and infrastructure inspection, with particular attention to railway/rolling-stock applications):

RQ1 (Integrated Architectures): To what extent does the terminological co-occurrence of AR/MR, AI/DL-based defect inspection, and HITL correspond to their functional integration within a single industrial maintenance or infrastructure-inspection workflow?

RQ2 (Metadata and Capture Conditions): Among the retained studies, how frequently are capture conditions, including 6-DOF pose, viewpoint, and lighting, recorded, and how are these data reused for model improvement?

RQ3 (Feedback Persistence and Retraining): Among the retained studies, is human feedback limited to session-level verification or correction, or is it persistently stored and reused through structured, recurring model-retraining cycles?

RQ4 (Architectural Hierarchy): Do the retained integrated systems document a hierarchical, morphology-matched multi-model architecture, or do they apply a unified model across defect types?

RQ5 (Sector Distribution): What is the distribution of integrated systems across sectors (railway/pantograph, aerospace, civil engineering), and what domain-specific factors influence architecture?

RQ6 (Outcomes and ROI): What operational outcomes and multidimensional benefits are reported for functionally integrated AR–AI–HITL inspection systems within the retained corpus?

The search string combined the three technology domains conjunctively: ("Augmented Reality" OR "Mixed Reality" OR AR OR MR) AND ("Deep Learning" OR AI) AND ("defect detection" OR segmentation) AND ("Human-in-the-Loop" OR HITL) AND (maintenance OR inspection OR infrastructure). Whereas broader reviews have examined AR adoption and implementation strategies across maintenance applications (Tayyarat et al., 2025b), the present search was deliberately intersection focused. It was designed to identify candidate studies situated at the terminological intersection of AR/MR, AI/DL-based defect inspection, and HITL, and to determine through full-text analysis whether this co-occurrence corresponded to functional integration, persistent feedback, and reuse of acquisition context. The resulting corpus therefore supports conclusions about the depth and form of integration among the retained studies.

Eligibility was assessed in three sequential phases (the overall selection process results are summarized in Fig. 2).

During Phase 1, the language, publication type, publication period, and field criteria, together with the exclusion of patents, were applied through the search-tool filters. The additional retention criteria for grey literature identified through Google Scholar were applied manually.

The inclusion criteria were as follows. IC-P1a: Only publications written in English were considered. IC-P1b: The eligible publication types included peer-reviewed journal articles, conference proceedings, book chapters, and qualified grey literature, such as technical reports, theses, and preprints. IC-P1c: The publication period was restricted to studies published between 2017 and 2026. IC-P1d: The studies had to fall within the field of Computer Science and Engineering.

The exclusion criteria were defined as follows. EC-P1a: Patents were excluded. Grey-literature records lacking a recognized issuing body, a described methodology of sufficient rigor, or a stable and accessible identifier were also excluded.

During Phase 2, the titles and abstracts of the retrieved publications were screened according to more specific eligibility criteria.

For inclusion, IC1 required the study to provide sufficient technical description of the AR/MR, AI/DL, and human-involvement components mentioned in the retrieved record to permit subsequent classification of their relationship as co-occurring, partially coupled, or functionally integrated. IC2 required the application domain to involve industrial maintenance or infrastructure inspection. IC3 required the study to address at least one acquisition-context factor, such as viewpoint, spatial registration, lighting, occlusion, device pose, or capture configuration (IC3 did not require persistent recording of 6-DOF pose or reuse of acquisition metadata for model improvement; these characteristics were coded separately under RQ2). IC4 required an explicit form of human involvement in inspection, verification, annotation, correction, or decision support (IC4 did not require persistent feedback storage or offline retraining; these characteristics were coded separately under RQ3). The exclusion criteria were applied to studies that presented EC1: isolated AI components without integration with AR/MR; EC2: visualization methods that were not based on AR/MR, including VR-only, desktop-based, or mobile approaches without a spatial overlay; and EC3: applications addressing general domains unrelated to inspection.

Phase 3, full-text assessment, verified that the AR/MR-, AI/DL-, and human-involvement concepts identified during screening were technically described in the maintenance or inspection context. The relationship among these components was then classified as terminological co-occurrence, partial functional coupling, or full functional integration. Selection bias was managed by applying predefined Phase 2 criteria to ensure reproducibility. Screening was performed primarily by the first author, with borderline inclusion/exclusion decisions informally reviewed with a co-author to resolve ambiguity. Temporal bias was controlled by bounding the search to a single reference date; language bias is acknowledged, as the search was restricted to English-language publications.

Data extraction, applied to each eligible record, covered bibliographic characteristics (publication year, venue, document type). For the 25 studies retained in the final corpus, extraction was extended to capture architecture-level detail. This included the AR/MR device and capture mode, the AI model and its role within the pipeline (detection versus segmentation), the HITL mechanism and correction type (session-level versus persistent retraining), whether capture metadata or pose was recorded, reported quantitative outcomes, and limitations relative to the architecture proposed in this paper.
